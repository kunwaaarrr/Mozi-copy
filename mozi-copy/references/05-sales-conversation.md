# 05 — The Sales Conversation: Question-Led Selling

The generalized framework for any sales conversation (call, Zoom, in person, or adapted into long-form copy like a VSL/landing page). The **Diagnostic Sale** — the gym version of this — appears at the end as one fully worked example; it is an *instance* of this framework, not the framework itself. Hormozi applied the same skeleton to med spas, agencies, lead-gen, and software ("we 10x'd recurring revenue… we didn't change what we delivered, we only changed how we presented it"). *(yt/02)*

---

## The universal skeleton

> Current state → Desired state → Obstacle (why aren't you there?) → Present the desired state, with your vehicle as the way past the obstacle → tie the price to the *goal*, not the product → incentive to prepay. *(yt/02)*

Expanded into the conversation flow:

1. **Pre-frame** — questionnaire before the meeting (raises their problem-awareness and arms you), collect payment details/ID early where possible, and kill the decision-maker zombie up front: *"Before we start — is this a decision you can make on your own, or is there someone else's thumbs-up you'd need? [If so] We just want them here too so we can all decide together."* *(gym-launch/11, 15; yt/02)*
2. **Agenda / frame control** — set it in one breath: proof, promise, plan. *"[We've helped X people like you get Y.] Here's what we'll do: I'll ask some questions, and at the end if it makes sense to move forward, great — if not, no sweat, I'll still send you off with stuff that helps."* Setting the agenda = controlling the frame = looking like a professional. (A multi-million-dollar closer's favorite move: "Can we skip the foreplay?" — get to the point, especially B2B.) *(yt/01)*
3. **Goal** — "What brought you in? What's the goal? What are you trying to accomplish?" (ask the cluster; people need multiple angles to open up). Then **recap it back**: *"So you're at [X], you want to get to [Y] — got it. We do this all the time."* Repeating back what they said = they feel heard = rapport on fast-forward. The live calls do this constantly: "So if I'm hearing you…" *(gym-launch/11, 16)*
4. **Why now?** — *"Why now? What changed? Why today and not yesterday, or last month?"* They state their own urgency — which pre-kills the stall objection an hour before it would appear. *(gym-launch/11)*
5. **Deeper why** — *"Why is this important to you? What is [the problem] preventing you from doing right now that you want to do?"* This is the emotional payload you'll reuse at the close, in their own words. *(gym-launch/11)*
6. **The pain cycle / gap** — *"What have you done before? What did you like about it? What was missing? Why do you think it didn't stick?"* Two rules: **everything they've tried is an advantage** — it didn't solve the problem, that's why they're here ("I already have a personal trainer." — "He's obviously not doing a good job… you're here." — "…fair.") — and you're listening for which of your **three pillars** was missing so the pitch writes itself. *(yt/01)*
7. **Future pace** — *"Ideal outcome: 12 months from now, what would have to happen for you to look back and say this was one of the best decisions you've made? How does daily life look different?"* (Read the person: a no-nonsense engineer may roll eyes at this — judge and adapt.) *(gym-launch/11)*
8. **Preference questions (self-prescription)** — a short series of either/or questions about *how* they want it delivered — each answer affirmed: "Smart move." "Good call." "Totally makes sense." Then **recap their own answers back as the program**. They designed it; you just priced it. *(gym-launch/11)*
9. **Flip the close** — *"Now that you understand how this would work — why do you think you'd be a **good fit**? Why do you think this would work for you?"* They sell themselves out loud (Law 3: they believe what *they* say). Then: *"I really do think so too. So where do you want to go from here?"* — **the whole goal is that they ask you the price.** *(gym-launch/11)*
10. **Timeline + guarantee → price** — see below.
11. **Close, then shut up** — once they're in: take payment, stop selling. "Do not sell yourself out of a sale… save your Lego Star Wars lightsaber for after she gets back." New details after a yes can only turn it back into a maybe. *(yt/01)*

## The pitch itself: three pillars, two minutes, metaphors

- **Pitch length: under 3 minutes.** ("Our sales pitch on the call is under three minutes. 180 seconds.") You talk about the prospect 90% of the time, the product 10%. *(yt/02)*
- **Chunk your offer up to exactly three pillars** (whatever the real feature count — merge until three; humans remember threes). Fitness: fitness / nutrition / accountability. Lead-gen: timely / qualified / exclusive. Find yours by asking "what are the three things an amazing [outcome] requires?" — then the pain cycle (step 6) tells you which pillar they've always been missing: *"So you had nutrition and accountability with Weight Watchers — but no fitness. You lost the weight and gained it back because you lost muscle. That's why it didn't work — all we have to do is fix this one thing."* If you have fitness, nutrition, and accountability, you can't fail. / If your leads are timely, qualified, and exclusive, you make money. *(yt/01)*
- **State the pillar, then give the metaphor** (a metaphor compares the thing they don't understand to a thing they do — that's what teaching is). The proven ones:
  - **Three legs of a stool** — two legs balances briefly, then falls over. "You lost 50, gained 60 — it balanced, then it fell."
  - **The toothbrush** (accountability) — "Did you hate brushing your teeth as a kid? Your parent reminded you every night — external accountability. Now you do it daily without fail — internal motivation. We're going to be that external accountability until the habit pays for itself." *(also live in gym-launch/15)*
  - **The Amazing Race / local in the backseat** (coaching) — the couple who kept winning hired a local in every new city. "I've been where you're going, with a hundred people just like you — let me be your backseat driver. It's an unfair advantage."
  - **Netflix vs Blockbuster** (convenience/sustainability) — "When's the last time you had to get motivated to push play on Netflix? That's how your [workouts] should feel."
  - **Income vs investment account** (paid ads vs SEO; anything fast-vs-compounding) — "Ads are your paycheck: work in, money out now. SEO is your investment account: slow, then it compounds past anything you could do actively."
  - **Prius → Ferrari** (metabolic build-up; any capacity-building) — "Right now you take in a little and burn a little. We're going to slowly build you up to a Ferrari."
  - **Insurance policy** (coaching/guarantee) — "The accountability coach is insurance: if you fall off the road, the policy gets you back on. The best athletes in the world still have coaches."
  *(yt/01, gym-launch/11, 15, 16)*
- **Sell the vacation.** Maui, not TSA. The transformation, never the logistics. When a prospect asks about the bullets on the page — you don't talk about the bullets on the page. *(yt/01, yt/02)*

## Pricing psychology

- **They set the goal → the price came from them.** Like frozen-yogurt shops: you fill your own cup, so the number on the scale is *your* doing. Have them self-identify current state and pick the target ("where are you on this chart?" / scale / metric). Then the timeline and price are just arithmetic on *their* goal, and the price is never about your product: *"You're not buying [filler/training/leads] — you're buying [the way heads turn / the bikini / the freedom]."* *(yt/02)*
- **Price the goal, not the months.** *"Given you came in to lose 30 pounds — I think we can both agree [the 6-week promo] isn't going to get you there, and definitely not keep it off… unless we cut off body parts (ha)."* Then: it takes [N weeks to achieve] + [~half N to make it stick/maintain] → total timeline → rate × timeline = total. The maintenance/recovery phase both is true and doubles the contract. *(gym-launch/11)*
- **Anchor high with a vehicle menu** (flight classes to the same destination): *"Private jet — fully one-on-one — $750/week. First class — everything individualized, you train with a few others — $99/week. Economy — $77. The party bus — $49 — fun, but it'll take a heck of a lot longer."* Recommend the one that fits what they told you ("Given what you said, first class is your best bet"). Anchoring at the jet makes $99 feel sensible; one in five takes a far higher tier than you'd guess. *(gym-launch/13, 11)*
- **The cash cascade** (each step only if the previous one stalls):
  1. **Pay in full −20%**: "So what most people do is prepay and save 20%. **Fair enough?**"
  2. **Half down −10%**: "No worries — you can put half down today and still snag 10% off, plus savings on every payment after."
  3. **Small down + weekly**: "Cool — how about $599 down to get you started, and we split the rest over the [N] weeks → $X/week."
  4. **Minimum down**: "We just always need at least $X to start — *that's just how we've always done it*." (One of the best overcomes ever; it works on almost everyone.)
  5. **Shorter program**: "How about we just start you with eight weeks and go from there — sound fair?"
  
  This cascade is why the math works: ~1 in 5 closes at the full-ticket PIF, ~1 in 5 at half down, the rest at $600–1,000 down — which 4x'd average upfront cash ($200 → $850+) and let ad spend be recouped same-day. *(gym-launch/11, 13)*
- **"Sound fair?" / "Fair enough?" — the lowest possible bar.** Never "sound great?" — they may not agree it's great, but *everyone* agrees it's fair, and a yes here is the close: "Awesome — what card do you want to use?" Say it with the pitch rising at the end so it demands a response. *(gym-launch/13)*
- **Payment-plan choice frame:** "We have two types of payment plans: either *we* choose how much you pay and when, or *you* choose how much you pay and when. Which works best for you?" → "I want to pick" → "Great choice. Most people just put it on a card so they grab the 20% savings and then pay on their own budget and schedule. Fair enough?" *(gym-launch/13)*
- **The ID close:** before payment, ask for their **ID** ("and is the zip on your ID the same as the card you'll be using? I'll trade you" — gesture at the wallet). Once the wallet is on the table, "I forgot my card" can't happen, the chit-chat over the ID photo builds rapport, and the details are right. Zoom version: "Can you hold your ID up to the camera? We just have to make sure you're you." If pressed: "That's just how we've always done it." *(yt/01)*
- **Never cut price to close.** Price drops between customers poison everything (people talk), and "can you do it for less?" is a *buying signal*. Counter-anchor instead — the response and reasoning live in `06-objection-closes.md` § Money. *(yt/01)*

## The guarantee (structure + how to present it)

*"I can guarantee the result in [N weeks] — **provided** you [the conditions: respond to my messages, hit your check-ins, send the daily photo, take what I tell you to take, follow the plan]. If you do all of that and don't hit it, I keep working with you until you do, free of charge. Sound fair?"*

- Conditions make the guarantee *theirs to keep alive* — and each condition is also the delivery mechanism that makes them succeed.
- Set realistic targets (~2 lbs/week or equivalent) so the guarantee almost never triggers; if servicing continues past the deadline, recurring product/supplement purchases keep it paid for anyway. *(gym-launch/11, 23, 14)*
- Best-case/worst-case framing of a guarantee is the single strongest close in the library (see `06-objection-closes.md` § Self).

## Worked example: the Diagnostic Sale (fitness)

The condensed full script — every line above, instantiated. Two pages, printed on a clipboard with checkbox dots so it reads as an intake questionnaire, not a pitch; a brand-new salesperson closed $2,400 on their first try reading it. After it replaced four different selling systems: upfront cash 4x'd, 100% of buyers entered recurring billing, close rates went 30% → 70–80% (one rep: <25% → 15-of-17 first try, $200 → $1,300 average ticket), churn fell (prepaid members don't quit; one-on-one relationships are hard to quit), half the sales appointments, and $39/week → $99/week pricing. *(gym-launch/10, 11)*

> Decision-maker check → "What brought you in? What's the goal?" → recap ("you're 205, want 150 — we do this all the time") → "Why now — what changed?" → "Why is it important — what's it stopping you doing?" → "Why didn't it work before — what was missing?" [diagnose missing pillar, tell its story/metaphor] → "12 months from now, what makes this the best decision you've made?" → "Do you know how the program works? Everything's custom to you — that's why we guarantee results. I'll ask a few questions, you tell me which you prefer:" gym or home? *smart move* / set times or convenience? *great choice* / template or custom plan? *good call* / same meals or variety? *makes sense* / group or one-on-one? *smart decision* → recap their answers as the program → "Why do you think you'd be a good fit?" → "So where do you want to go from here?" → [they ask price] → goal-priced timeline + guarantee → menu anchor → cash cascade → "Sound fair? Great — grab your ID and we'll get you started."

**Live-call moves worth stealing** *(gym-launch/15–18)*:
- **Reflective listening as the engine**: "So if I'm hearing you…" before every transition; prospects do 80% of the talking.
- **Damaging admission** (Tom Miller call): "We *used* to be a quick-fix program like the others… turns out people plateaued. The ones who always made progress had a one-on-one coach, so we changed our whole model." Honesty about a past weakness buys enormous trust.
- **Permission + vulnerability**: "Can I be super transparent on something? I feel like you haven't given yourself permission to fully visualize your ideal body… for the rest of this meeting, you have permission."
- **"Results love speed"** — bridging their arrival into action: "You saw it, you're here, you filled out the application. If it's a good fit, let's get going — make sense?"
- **Prospect's own language as the close**: Chad's prospect said "math over emotion" — he crowned it ("you're the first person I've talked to that *gets* that") and reused it at the ask.
- **Story proof at the decision point** (Bob story): "You totally remind me of Bob — his picture's right there. Highly reluctant to start, needed the accountability… within a year he lost 100 pounds. Now he's doing Spartan races, planking with his kids on his back. Thank goodness he went for it."
- **Future-pace in their currency**: the retiring couple — "you want to sell the house, buy an Airstream, and have no address… and if you can't walk 50 yards without sitting down, you'll be pissed off the whole time. You don't want to be limited — you want to be *limitless*."
- **Fork in the road**: "You're at a fork: this way you already know what it looks like — diabetes, heart disease. This way is totally under your control. I'll tell you exactly what to do — but it's you who gets to decide."
- **Make the ask binary and tiny**: "Which one fits you better — do you want to break it into payments, or save quite a bit and pay in full?"
