# 06 — The Close Library: Every Objection, Organized

All objections are the prospect casting their power to one of three things (the Ellis taxonomy from `01-core-principles.md` Law 21): **Circumstances** (time / money / fit-details), **Others** (spouse, partner, boss), or **Self** (fear, doubt, stalling). They usually present outside-in; every layer you peel is a step closer to the real conversation. Wrap *every* close below in the 3A reframe (Acknowledge → Associate → Ask, `07-reframing-and-negotiation.md`) and never deliver one like a fired bullet — "chew on the words; taste them before you say them" (`08-delivery-voice-mindset.md`).

**Operating rules for any objection:**
- **Always agree first.** "Totally understand." You're never in a fight; if you win the argument you lose the sale.
- **Looping is legal and unlimited**: resolve the concern → ask again. You can ask as many times as you like *as long as each ask follows a resolution*. Asking again without resolving is what annoys people. *(yt/03)*
- **Stack closes.** When they're teetering, hit the same obstacle from two or three angles in a row (e.g., busy → priorities-not-time → macro-seasons → "we're cutting 90% of what doesn't work"). Know the *concepts*; the words then mix and match themselves. Don't memorize lines — understand them. *(yt/01, gym-launch/20)*
- **These only work if they feel fluid and natural.** Practiced so well you can say them as you feel them. *(gym-launch/20)*

---

## 0 · Isolation questions (run these before any close)

When you hear "I need to think about it" or any vague no, *don't close yet — isolate*:
- **"Totally understand — what's your main concern?"** (early in the conversation)
- **"What are you afraid of having happen?"** / "What's the worst thing you envision?" (later; the answer is the *real* obstacle). If they're not sure, fill in absurdly: *"I take your credit card, swipe it for as much as I can, fly to Vegas, put it all on black… then Monaco, where they can't extradite me. Fair enough?"* — they laugh, then tell you the real thing.
- **"What are the variables you're using to make the decision?"**
- **"What would make this a yes?"** (if stuck: "can I share the decision variables I usually hear?")
- **"What makes this a no?"** — people are terrified to ask this; it's gold. "I don't really know" → *"That's amazing — sounds like you're just nervous about making the decision, which makes you exactly like the people who buy and get amazing results, because it shows you care. Ready to rock and roll?"*
*(yt/01)*

## 1 · All-purpose closes (work on any objection)

- **"The reason" close:** take whatever they said, append: *"Do you think the fact that [their objection] is the reason you haven't succeeded so far — and why you're here talking to me today?"* ("It's a lot of money" → "Do you think not investing in the past is the reason you're not where you want to be?" "I have to ask my spouse" → "Do you think always needing to ask is part of why this hasn't happened yet?") They're here *because* they haven't solved it; their objection is usually how. *(yt/01)*
- **The 1-to-10 close:** *"Scale of 1 to 10 — 1 being you want to [stay exactly as you are, worst version], 10 being this is a huge priority and you want to change it today — where are you?"* Whatever they say: *"Why is it a [7.5] and not a 2?"* (they list their own reasons — let them; add prompts: kids? role model? marriage/energy?) → *"So what stops it from being a 10?"* → reframe their gap (e.g., the airplane-mask: "what if doing this one thing for *you* is what lets you be the better [mom/leader/partner] you just described — wouldn't that make it a 10?") → *"Well, if it's a 10, it's a priority. Let's do it."* Also the quick variant: "what would make it a 10?" → fix that one thing → *"Are we feeling 10-ish?"* *(gym-launch/21, yt/01)*
- **Best case / worst case** (strongest with any guarantee): *"You've got two options, and both are risk-free. Walk out and do nothing — guaranteed to keep you exactly where you are. Or take a shot — also risk-free [guarantee] — with a real chance of getting everything you said you want. Only one of these is guaranteed to fail. Which risk-free option do you want?"* "I've probably closed more sales on best-case/worst-case than any other close I have." *(yt/01, yt/02)*
- **The mirror close:** *"The reason you're telling yourself not to do this **is** the reason you need to do it."* No time → that's exactly why. Can't afford it → how long do you want "I can't afford things" on your list of problems? Need permission → that dependence is the thing to fix. Their excuse is the chain; break it and the power comes back. *(yt/02)*
- **Closer-or-further:** *"Forget perfect. Do you think doing this gets you closer to your goal, or further away? …Closer? Then what are we waiting for — we don't need to be snipers, we need to be directionally right."* *(yt/02)*

## 2 · Circumstances — Time

- **Macro (seasonal) busy:** *"Do you want this to last long-term? …Do you think you'll be busy again in the future? …Then isn't it best to start while you're busy? If you learn it busy, you keep it forever; if it only works in perfect circumstances, you lose it the moment life gets loud again — and isn't that exactly when you'd want the most support?"* *(yt/02)*
- **Micro (no hours in the day) — the phone story:** *"I used to complain I had no time — my wife got so sick of it she pulled up my phone's screen time. 22 hours a week on social media. Turns out I had time; I just had to stop doing the stuff that wasn't working. That's the first thing we do — cut the 90% that's eating your day for nothing. Jeff Bezos gets the same 24 hours."* It's priorities, not time. *(yt/02, yt/05)*
- **When-then fallacy:** "When I have [time/money], then I'll start" flips the sequence — like *"I'll go to the hospital when I get better"* or *"I'll start saving money once I'm rich."* The program is the thing that produces the time/money they're waiting for. Empathize, don't attack: "Totally understand — I was stuck like this for years," then walk them through the epiphany. *(yt/02)*
- **"Give me a week to decide"** is not a time objection — it's avoidance (§5). "I'm busy right now" = priorities (above). Diagnose before closing. *(yt/01)*

## 3 · Circumstances — Money

- **"Is this a lot of money to you?"** — the gasp response. "No" → "Awesome, then let's go." "Yes" → *"That's exactly why you're going to succeed. Other people buy this and aren't all-in. The question is whether you want to draw the line and go all in — because this isn't about whether it works; I've proven that."* The fact that it's a lot for you is *good* — it means you'll actually do it. (Twin from the gym version: "If the money means a lot to you, then you can't afford to lose it — which means you'll finally show up, which is why you'll finally get the result. The money is there for you, not for me. If it were $10, you wouldn't care, you wouldn't show, you wouldn't change.") *(yt/02, gym-launch/26)*
- **Worth-it sidestep:** *"If all this did was [add $10K/month / get you in the bikini], would it be worth it? …Then it's not about the price — it's about whether you believe me. Let's talk about that."* Converts a money objection into a proof conversation, which you can win (testimonials: "How many people would you need to see for it to be more unreasonable NOT to believe? 10? 50? Here's 1,000 — tell me when to stop."). *(yt/02)*
- **Pay in time or pay in money:** *"You're going to spend this money over the next 12 months either way — and you're going to buy this program either way. The only question is whether you pay in money or pay in time. You'll learn every lesson in here eventually — do you want it to take 12 weeks or 12 years? …And being real: you've been paying in time for the last six years. How's that working out?"* (The personal version: "the only way to buy time in this life is to buy other people's mistakes — Edison spent years on the light bulb; we just buy the lesson.") *(yt/02)*
- **Resourceful, not resources:** *"What's the difference between a self-made billionaire when they started and you right now? Zero — you were both broke. So it was never about resources; it was about being resourceful."* The Shoe Dog story: Phil Knight, maxed out at every bank, got his *vendors* to cover Nike's payroll rather than fold. And the trap question for owners: *"Ever had a surprise tax bill? …And you're still alive — you magically found the money. You're resourceful when it's for someone else. Choose to be resourceful for you."* *(yt/02)*
- **Priorities, not budget:** *"I don't think you have a budget issue — I think you have a priorities issue. If [your kid needed therapy for his knee], you'd find the money, right? That's the situation your [health/business] is in right now. We vote with our dollars on the things we care about — and so far you've been voting for [the purse, the nails, eating out] and calling [this] the priority. Let's cast the first real vote."* *(gym-launch/26)*
- **"We always make it work":** *"95% of the people who sit in front of me are over budget — and it hasn't stopped them, because if you're anything like me, when we get something we can't quite afford… we always make it work, don't we? Extra shift, cut back somewhere. You're still here, still housed, still fed. If you really want this, you'll make it work — so let's do it."* *(gym-launch/26)*
- **The comparison close** ("I could go to [cheap competitor] for $29/month"): *"Totally — and if our thing and their thing cost the same, which would you pick? …Why?"* — and they list everything that makes you better, out of *their* mouth: the plan, the coach, the support, the guarantee. *"…So now you know why ours is more. And that's also why you've been going there two years and you're here talking to me."* (Identical mechanism as the B2B "cheap competitor close": "if ours was the same price as theirs, which would you do? why? …right. That's why we're more." It's usually a buying question — you're close.) *(gym-launch/26, yt/01)*
- **Now-vs-later (the cost of waiting):** *"It's cheaper to get healthy now than to pay the price of getting healthy later. You're sitting here today because you didn't do this four years ago. Wait more, and it costs more — more weight, more issues, more money — and you'll have spent the whole time not enjoying any of the benefits. You need this either way: do it now, save the money, start enjoying it sooner."* (Variant with stakes: "Have you priced out what it costs to keep buying bigger clothes? You're throwing away a closet you already paid for.") *(gym-launch/26)*
- **The ego check** (use selectively, with rapport — lands best on proud prospects): *"Really? That's surprising — I'd have taken you for someone where this wouldn't be an issue. Is it really the money, or is it something else?"* → either the truth comes out or "I can afford it" → "Awesome — then what's stopping us?" *(gym-launch/26)*
- **Past purchases / treat yourself:** *"Have you bought anything recently you couldn't really afford? …You made that work for a [purse]. Let's make it work for the thing you actually said you want."* If they claim they never have: *"Then you work so hard and never treat yourself — don't you think you deserve to actually get what you want for once? Sometimes it's worth paying more to get what you want than paying less to make another mistake."* And the gentle judo on past spending: *"Just because some past purchases put you in this spot doesn't mean you should penalize your current self and your future self by skipping the one that fixes it."* *(gym-launch/26)*
- **"Can you do it for less?" → "We could do it for more."** Perfect record. It counter-anchors (their 20%-off request now sits next to a +20% number), creates FOMO on the *current* price, and signals total conviction. Never lower price to close — change **terms** (the cascade in `05-sales-conversation.md`), never the number. Test prices premeditatedly, not mid-sale. *(yt/01)*

## 4 · Circumstances — Fit, preferences, details

- **New identity, new priorities** (the Sephora story): two girls, ~13, at the makeup counter, thrilled, and the saleswoman says "now that you're becoming women, you'll budget for this every month" — and they were *excited*, because **new identity creates new priorities**. *"Your current priorities belong to your old identity — the you that [drinks/scrolls/spends on X]. There's also the you that you want to be. People who want to be [fit/rich] spend on [their body/their skills]. Do you want to keep walking the road you're on, or step into the new identity — the way those girls did?"* Then the pointed version: *"You're wearing a $1,000 outfit. Would another $1,000 outfit make you look better — or your high-school weight?"* *(yt/02)*
- **The hypothetical-perfect:** *"If it were perfect, what's missing? What would the unicorn version look like?"* If they name something fixable: *"If I did green beans instead of broccoli, would you do it? …Great. Green beans it is. Sign here."* If they can't name anything: see unicorn close (§5). *(yt/02)*
- **Details are where you die — question the question:** never answer a detail straight (Law 11). *"How many certifications do your trainers have?"* → *"Which certifications are you looking for specifically?"* → "I don't know" → *"Sounds like you want to be sure you're making a safe, informed decision — fair? What I can tell you is [the chunk-level truth: every trainer triple-certified, zero injuries in two years]. Feel better? Great."* Find the *reason behind* the detail and close on the reason: support-email caps → "if you fully understood exactly what to do, would you even have questions? That's what we make sure of." *(yt/01)*
- **Mechanic / secretary close** (deflecting premature spec questions): *"Asking me that now is like asking the heart surgeon's receptionist what's wrong with your heart — or asking the mechanic what's wrong with the car before we've looked under the hood. It's going to depend — and honestly, anyone who gives you an answer **before** looking is just trying to sell you something. Run the other way from those people."* Moral high ground acquired; details exited. *(yt/01)*
- **Change the variables, change the outcome** (they want your result, their way): *"You can have my outcome with my process, or your process and the outcome it's already given you. Eating what you're eating is what got the body you've got. You've tried a lot of ways — your way included — and you're here. If you're willing to do it this way, these are the numbers we get. Sound fair?"* Lower the tone; sound reasonable, not combative. *(yt/01)*
- **"I'll just do your free stuff / what you said on this call":** *"You absolutely should — before we kick you off. Do it, and one of two things happens: it works, and you hit the next problem up, which we help you solve — or it doesn't, and we help you solve that. Either way you end up doing this, so let's get started."* *(yt/01)*
- **"You don't have a guarantee":** anti-guarantee — the lack of guarantee *is* the filter/commitment device; frame it as "we only work with people who don't need an exit." *(yt/01 — partial; passage truncated in source)*

## 5 · Self — Stalls, fear, avoidance ("I need to think about it")

Reaching "self" is *good news*: every outside excuse is exhausted and you're finally talking to the person in power. Frame: past / present / future.

- **Thought is instantaneous:** *"Think of a purple monkey. …Instant, right? Thinking is instantaneous — and so is deciding. What takes time is avoiding the decision. Deciding two days from now takes the same five seconds it takes right now — except you won't be sitting in a rocking chair contemplating it. The phone rings, kids need pickup, groceries, work — and I am heartbroken to report you will not be thinking about me. So 'thinking about it' really means actively deciding to delay — and we both know what delayed decisions become."* *(gym-launch/21)*
- **Unicorn close:** *"Help me out — what does the unicorn program you're looking for have that this doesn't? You need [pillar 1]? Covered. [2]? Covered. [The plan, the book, the coach, the list]? Covered, covered, covered. So what's missing? …Nothing? Then the only thing left standing between you and the goal… is you. We're standing right here, door open. Stop knocking and running away."* (People laugh at "unicorn" — that's the point.) *(gym-launch/21)*
- **Consider-the-options (logic walk):** list every alternative and let each one die: *"Leave here and you still want [outcome]. Option A: [premium alternative] — ~$1,500, no guarantee, none of [your pillars]. Option B: [the thing they already tried] — you know how that goes: pay money to be 10 pounds heavier in six months. Want that? Option C: [$29 cheap option] — you already don't go, and even if you magically did, there's no [plan/support], so you'd quit with no results. Or: this. Same money or less, all three pillars, guarantee. If you want to **stop trying** to [lose weight] and **start actually** [losing weight] — let's do it."* The risk-free version when you have a guarantee: *"Three options. Do it and it works — life's awesome. Don't do it — guaranteed to stay exactly here. Do it and it doesn't work — guarantee, money back. Two of those are risk-free and one of them is guaranteed to fail. Which risk-free option do you want?"* *(gym-launch/21, yt/02)*
- **Don't let it burn you twice** (bad past experience): *"You let a bad decision burn you twice — once when you made the bad investment, and a second time when you let it stop you from a good one. It's like having a lousy eighth-grade boyfriend and concluding 'men aren't for me.' There are bad coaches and good ones — and judging the good by the bad costs you exactly what the bad one did, again."* (Receipts: the woman who texted six years later — "I'm kicking myself for not jumping on.") *(yt/02)*
- **Time ≠ information:** *"People think they need more time to decide because they assume more information will arrive in that time. But I'm the source of the information — it's all right here. So let's get you everything you need now, and then the decision takes five seconds."* *(yt/02)*
- **The informed-decision close** (with any trial/guarantee): *"You can't actually make an informed decision from out there — only from the inside. So I'm not asking you to decide forever; I'm asking you to make an *informed* decision, which you can only do once you're in. 30 days, and if I didn't do what I said — money back. You get to decide by not deciding."* *(yt/02)*
- **Decadere** (for logic-brains): *"Know where 'decide' comes from? Latin — decadere: to cut off, to kill. Every decision kills a future. So which future are we killing today — the one with your dreams in it, or the one that looks exactly like the last five years? Because indecision is a decision. Inaction is an active choice."* *(yt/02)*
- **Magnify the future pain:** *"It's been five years of this. How does another five sound? Play it out with me — five more years of exactly what you've been doing. Where's the weight? Where's the business? Is that somewhere you want to be? …Then we've got to change the change."* *(yt/02)*
- **Might-as-well-now:** *"You're not going to struggle forever — someday you'll fix this, right? Then if you're going to do it eventually, do it now and start enjoying the result sooner. Faster or slower — what's your preference?"* *(yt/02)*
- **What are you afraid of? + Let's play it out** (the master fear-confrontation): isolate the fear (§0), then: *"Let's play it out. Best case: [their stated dream]. Worst case: [the actual worst case, said out loud — usually survivable, often funny]."* The Dave Ramsey envelope lady: "If I give you this envelope, that's my grocery money." → "What are you afraid of — not affording food? …You're trying to lose weight. Worst case, you can't buy food and you lose weight. Win-win." (Said with warmth — she laughed.) → *"Real talk: ever bought something you weren't sure you could afford? And here you are — still alive, still housed. We always make it work in the end. Best case you change your life forever. That a bet you're willing to take?"* *(yt/02, yt/03)*
- **"The goal seems too aggressive / I can't hit that"** (goal-skepticism): the damaging-admission answer — *"The goal actually used to be lower — and basically everyone hit it, but people were gaming it [water-loading the weigh-in, starving the weigh-out]. So we set the goal to account for the gaming, which means the real, tissue-level result is completely standard — about two pounds a week. That's why the success rate is still obscene **and** the transformations are real."* Generalize: when a target looks scary, explain the engineering behind the number so it reads as *designed*, not hyped. *(gym-launch/23)*

## 6 · Others — Spouse / partner / decision-maker

Hardest category — it's the only one where they claim to have *no authority at all*. Prevention first: kill it in the pre-frame ("is this a decision you can make on your own?"), and for couples/B2B get both decision-makers in the room — it routinely turns into *two* sales ("you get better results together"). For enterprise multi-stakeholder sales, much of the one-call playbook doesn't apply — bring stakeholders in. *(yt/01, gym-launch/11)*

The four-step sequence when it surfaces anyway *(yt/01, gym-launch/22)*:

1. **"What if they say no?"** — One out of three times (genuinely) they answer "honestly, I'd probably do it anyway" → *"Then what are we waiting for? Let's get you started."* For proud men, the spicier version: "what happens if he doesn't give you *permission*?" — nobody wants to need permission. *(gym-launch/22, yt/01)*
2. **Break the foil** — if "then I wouldn't do it": *"What do you think would be their main concern — the money, or whether you'll get the result?"* They now negotiate *as* the absent spouse — who is usually a foil for their own concern — and whatever they name, you close that (§3 money or proof-stack) instead of fighting a ghost. *(gym-launch/22, yt/01)*
3. **Past agreements:** *"Does [he] know you're here? Does he know you've wanted this and struggled? Is he happy about how you feel right now? …So he doesn't approve of your current situation. Why would he disapprove of you fixing something he already doesn't approve of?"* Add the rules-reversed test: *"If the roles were reversed and this was his dream, would you support him? …Then why wouldn't you support you?"* And the personal-proof variant: *"If your husband's anything like my wife — she never tells me no on something she knows I really, really want. Do you really want this? …Then let's get you started. And honestly, sometimes it's better to ask forgiveness than permission"* *(said with a smirk — it's a tag-on, not a standalone).* *(gym-launch/22, yt/02)*
4. **Support, not permission** (the final straw, and the deepest one): *"I think you're asking for permission when what you actually need is support. It's your life, not theirs. Play it out: if you keep needing permission and two years from now nothing's changed — who do you blame? Them. That's how resentment gets built. So don't ask permission — tell them: 'If I don't do this for me, I'm going to end up resenting you, and I don't want that. Instead of complaining to you every day about [money/my body], I'm doing something about it. I'm not asking permission — I'm asking for your support.'"* Only say this if you believe it — it's true, and it has saved more than sales. *(yt/02, yt/01)*

**Humor pressure-release (high rapport only) — the Cheetos close:** *"Sign up, go home, tell him. And if he looks at you and says: 'Babe — I want you to live a shorter life. I want you to be a terrible example for our kids. I want you to sit on that couch, pull up the sweatpants, reach into the Cheetos bag and wipe your fingers on them, and never look better than you do right now' — you call me, because A, I need to talk to him, and B, I'll tear the contract up myself. Fair enough?"* Pair with a no-questions short guarantee ("3-day no-sweat") to remove the last excuse. *(gym-launch/22, yt/02)*

**B2B / business-partner variant:** steps 1–2 work verbatim ("what would happen if they said no?" / "what do you think would be their main concern?" — the source applies them explicitly to "my wife, my employees, my kid, my business partner"). For steps 3–4 the business framing from the source: *"Let's play it out — if the business doesn't grow, who are you going to blame? Is it fair to the partnership that you don't get this outcome because you rely on this person? If they're the ones in charge, they're the ones who get all the blame — which means you're not in control of your own outcomes. I don't think that's fair to you, to them, or to the partnership."* For genuinely shared authority (50/50 partners, enterprise stakeholders), prevention is the whole play: get everyone on the call. *(yt/01)*

**The Susan story** (play out past→future, told as a story rather than an argument): *"Let me tell you about Susan — same thing, 'I don't do anything without my husband's consent.' She walked out. Saw her a few years later: 'How's the fitness stuff going?' 'It's not. My husband never lets me do anything.' …That's the track this is on. You'll end up resenting him — which is why I think you're asking for permission when you need support."* *(yt/03)*

## 7 · Tactical / end-of-sale closes

- **Delayed-payment close** (the last resort — exhaust everything above first): *"I get it — so let's do this: we get you signed up now, and the first payment doesn't run until the start date. Between now and then, change your mind for any reason — just tell me, you pay nothing. This way your spot exists."* Defaults flip: deciding *not* to proceed now requires an action. Most never cancel. *(gym-launch/24)*
- **"I forgot my wallet"**: *"No problem — pull up your banking app, your account number's right on your statements, and we'll Google the routing number. We can do it as an ACH right now."* (One rep closes two extra sales a *day* with this.) Prevention is the ID close in `05-sales-conversation.md`. *(gym-launch/25)*
- **Objections-that-aren't** — "wow, that's expensive" said while reaching for the card: agree lightly ("totally — most things worth doing are") and *keep moving*. Don't unload closes on zombies that aren't there. *(yt/01)*
