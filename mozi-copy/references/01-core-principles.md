# 01 — Core Principles: The Operating System

Everything else in this skill hangs off this file. These are the beliefs and laws that govern every piece of copy, every script, and every conversation. Other files reference these laws by name instead of restating them.

---

## What selling (and sales copy) actually is

- **Sales is increasing the likelihood that the prospect buys.** Nothing more mystical. A perfect sales process would account for every variable and convert 100%. Everything in this skill exists to nudge that percentage up. *(yt/01)*
- **Sales is an education process — personalized marketing.** If a prospect knew everything you know about the product, they'd buy. Your job is to fill in only the gaps *that person* needs in order to decide. Marketing covers points 1–3; the conversation covers 4–6, customized per person. If you get them to buy with information that would make them NOT buy if they knew it, you deceived them. Don't. *(yt/01)*
- **Sales is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust.** Two requirements: you must believe (so there's something to transfer) and they must trust you (so it can cross). Hot streaks end not because you forgot how to sell but because you stopped believing in why. *(yt/02, gym-launch/28)*
- **Sales is the first step of coaching.** Selling properly is helping prospects make decisions to help themselves. You are not helping them — you're helping them help themselves. *(yt/02)*

## The laws (use these as a checklist on every draft)

1. **People want to buy. Help their logical brain justify the decision they already want to make.** Emotion decreases the action threshold and gets the step taken; logic makes it stick and makes a great customer. Answer both. *(yt/02)*
2. **Selling happens before you ask. Closing happens after.** The sale starts at the click/first impression, not at the conversation. Everything between click and call (speed, edification, reminders) changes close rates independent of what you say. *(yt/01, yt/02)*
3. **They believe nothing you say. They believe everything they say.** So ask questions that lead them to say it. The person asking the questions controls the conversation; the person answering is the one being interrogated. Be "like smoke" — answer questions with questions unless you already know your statement lands. Mechanics in `07-reframing-and-negotiation.md`. *(yt/02, yt/03)*
4. **Sell the vacation, not the flight. Sell the prize, not the program.** Nobody wants a 28-day transformation or a 42-day detox; they want the bikini, the energy, how their kids see them. Talk about Maui, never about TSA. *(gym-launch/10, yt/01)*
5. **Expect and plan for no. No is the job.** If they could decide alone they'd have just sent the money. Closing *begins* when someone says no — and one no is not no forever. *(yt/01, yt/02)*
6. **Obstacles before price, objections after.** It's far easier to "kill zombies" / defuse bombs up front (spouse, time, fit) than after you've named a number, when you've become a salesperson in their mind. Anything left unaddressed will blow up in the close. *(yt/02, yt/03)*
7. **Whoever cares most about the prospect wins the sale — including against the prospect themselves.** If you care more about their outcome than they do, and you're more convicted than they are in their excuse, you win. "The war of sales is won or lost on who believes more: do you believe in your product more than they believe in their excuse?" *(yt/02, gym-launch/28)*
8. **People buy from people they like and trust. Both, not either.** Like without trust = friendly but no results expected. Trust without like = competent but unpleasant. If they like you and trust you when you make the ask, it's done. *(gym-launch/28)*
9. **Seek to understand, not to win.** If you win the argument in a sale, you lose the sale. You become rich by making *them* right. Practice childlike curiosity ("Huh, that's interesting — tell me more about that"). Delivery mechanics in `08-delivery-voice-mindset.md`. *(yt/02, yt/03)*
10. **Listen twice as much as you talk.** Measured across a whole portfolio of recorded calls, the best closers had ~2:1 listen:talk. The best salespeople don't come off as salespeople; people don't feel sold — they feel like they bought. *(yt/03)*
11. **Details are where you die.** Never answer a detail question directly; question the question, find the reason behind it, chunk up, close on the reason. A detail obstacle is really a preference obstacle: "I want your results, my way" — and you can't have both ("change the variables, change the outcome"). Closes in `06-objection-closes.md`. *(yt/01)*
12. **Scarcity + urgency make people act.** Scarcity = limited spots ("only a couple left"). Urgency = limited time ("ends Friday"). Every CTA should carry at least one, ideally both — and you must mean it ("for real this time" is what moved show rates from 50% to 70%; story in `03-cold-calling.md`). *(gym-launch/06)*
13. **If you didn't get a gasp from the price, you didn't go high enough.** A high anchor makes everything after it feel like a rounding error. Never *lower* a price to close ("we don't negotiate with terrorists") — change terms, not price. *(yt/02, yt/01)*
14. **The bigger the plane, the longer the runway.** More expensive things need more education/warm-up before takeoff. If closes take 3–4 calls, the fix is more pre-education (content, case studies, proof), not more pressure. *(yt/01)*
15. **Volume negates luck. Speed is king; volume is its twin.** The number-one predictor of money made is number of reach-outs, and the faster the contact the more each one is worth. The full system and the math live in `02-outreach-and-nurture.md`. *(gym-launch/02, yt/01)*
16. **Word concision.** Don't take 100 words to say what 5 can. Shorter calls = more calls; shorter copy = read copy. Don't add ritual appendages just because something worked once (the "cough before the close" trap). *(yt/02)*
17. **Belief and trust are continuums, not binaries.** Not "do they trust me" but "how much?" Every element either adds to or subtracts from the running total. Stack adds. *(yt/01, yt/02)*
18. **Humans think in threes.** Chunk any pitch up to three pillars, whatever your actual feature count. Pitch construction in `05-sales-conversation.md`. *(yt/01)*
19. **An objection is not a no.** "Wow, that's expensive" is often just a reaction spoken aloud — people say "that's expensive" and then buy. Don't waste bullets on zombies that aren't there; unless they say they don't want to buy, don't assume it. *(yt/01)*
20. **Educate about the prospect, not the product.** Spend 90% of training/research time understanding the prospect's pains. Know where they are, where they want to go, what they've tried — then any product bridges the gap. *(yt/02)*
21. **Power: all objections are the prospect casting their power to something outside themselves.** This is the master taxonomy (from Albert Ellis, inventor of CBT — he reduced 11 distortions of reality to three):
    - **Circumstances** — time, money, fit/details ("I must get what I want when I want it")
    - **Others** — spouse, partner, boss ("other people must treat me fairly… they decide")
    - **Self** — fear, doubt, avoidance, "I need to think about it" ("I must do well or I am no good")
    
    Objections are *distortions of reality* — they aren't real, and the close is walking the person through that realization without attacking them. They usually present outside-in (circumstances → others → self), so each layer overcome is a step closer to the truth, and "I need to think about it" is *good news*: you're finally talking to the person in power. The complete close library is organized by this taxonomy in `06-objection-closes.md`. *(yt/02)*

## The buyer math

Out of 100 prospects: **~30 will buy** as long as the price is reasonable to them (their buying threshold is already above the price), **~50 need convincing** — this group is where skill lives, and **~20 will never buy** (don't take it personally; mostly they just don't value the thing enough). The difference between a 30% close rate and an 80% close rate is purely your ability to overcome obstacles and objections with the middle 50. *(gym-launch/19)*

## Ethics (non-negotiable, baked into all output)

- **State the facts and tell the truth.** If the truth plus all relevant facts wouldn't get a qualified person to buy, the offer is the problem, not the copy.
- **Only sell qualified prospects.** If someone genuinely can't benefit, don't sell them. Less-resourced ≠ unqualified.
- **Keep the human number one.** If you genuinely believe the product helps this person, you have an obligation to keep asking; if you only care about closing, they'll smell it ("commission breath") and you'll lose.
- The 3A framework's ethical rules are detailed in `07-reframing-and-negotiation.md`. *(yt/04)*
