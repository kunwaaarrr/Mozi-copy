# 02 — Outreach & Nurture: The Speed/Volume System

Everything between someone showing interest (opt-in, inbound lead, form fill) and them showing up for the conversation. This is the system: who to contact, when, how often. The actual *message copy* (texts, reminders) lives in `04-messaging-sms-whatsapp.md`; the phone script lives in `03-cold-calling.md`.

## The two pillars: Speed and Volume

### Speed is king
- **Contact a lead within 60 seconds of opt-in → 391% increase in close likelihood.** Past 5 minutes the chance of closing drops ~80%. **50% of prospects go with whoever responds first** — not the best, the first. "Lead quality went down" is almost always "leads weren't worked fast enough." *(yt/01)*
- Speed works because it's **proximity to pain and proximity to interest**: the further from the moment they raised their hand, the less aware of their pain they are. *(gym-launch/02)*
- Practical: alert on every new lead → **double dial immediately** (call, and if no pickup, immediately call again — pickup rates jump because it feels like a real person genuinely trying to reach them). Respect legal calling hours (e.g., US FTC: 8am–9pm). The faster you are, the *less* total work you do. *(gym-launch/02, 03)*
- **Offer "call now," not just "schedule."** A call-now option on the funnel beats a 3-day-out booking. When they call (or you call to qualify), say: *"Hey, I had an opening later today that just opened up — do you want the slot?"* Same-day appointments massively out-show future ones. *(yt/01)*
- **Pull up calls.** Booked Thursday, it's Monday, you have an open slot today? Drag it forward. Today always out-shows Thursday, and you free Thursday for someone else. If someone hasn't confirmed for today's slot, release it: *"Sorry I didn't see your confirmation — why don't we do the same time tomorrow?"* and fill today with someone responsive. *(yt/03)*

### Volume is king (the money math)
- **A call is worth ~$10. Roughly 50 calls = 1 sale** (tracked across hundreds of campaigns). Every dial = picking up a $10 bill. *(gym-launch/02)*
- Worked example: want $50K in 30 days at a $500 ticket → 100 sales → ~400 leads at 25% close → ~5,000 calls → **167 calls/day** (≈4 hours at 40/hr). You can forget everything else and just hit the number. *(gym-launch/02)*
- **Sell 7 days a week.** 2 extra days × 52 weeks = 104 extra selling days = minimum +29% — more in practice, because weekends are when consumers are free, weekend slots book same-day/next-day (higher show rates), and skipping weekends stacks Monday with no-shows. *(yt/01)*
- **The relationship between volume and skill is a flywheel**: the more you do, the better you get; the better you get, the more you do. *(yt/01)*

## The contact cadence (the proven schedule)

Treat *scheduled* and *unscheduled* leads the same — a self-booked appointment from "someone who clicked twice with their thumb while on the toilet" will not show unless they confirm, build rapport, and want to come. The only difference: with scheduled leads you confirm the time; with unscheduled you also get a time. *(gym-launch/01)*

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Double-dial immediately → if no answer, text. Repeat mid-day. Repeat before end of day. (3 contact attempts; "double-dial + text" counts as one attempt) |
| Day 2 | Same as Day 1 |
| Days 3–5 | One double-dial + text per day |
| Day ~10 | **Last-push / last-chance campaign** (below) |
| After | Only periodic large-scale campaigns / email blasts |

*(gym-launch/03)*

### The last push
Around day 10 (or ~14 days before a promotion ends), blast the whole unconverted list one more time with a light-touch scarcity play — "hey, must have missed you, maybe life got in the way…" framing plus last-chance-to-get-in. **The last push always pays for itself.** Message copy in `04-messaging-sms-whatsapp.md`. *(gym-launch/01)*

## Lead temperatures

From the text-only nurture system (a top rep sold ~200/month from ~1,000 leads, 70%+ show rate, three hours of texting a day, never calling):
- **Hot** — opted in *and* self-scheduled → confirm immediately + make them act ("text me right now to confirm — only a few spots left").
- **Warm** — opted in, didn't schedule → "still interested? text me right now…" then offer a same-day window.
- **Cool** — everyone else → last-push and periodic campaigns.

Use this triage on any channel. Verbatim scripts in `04-messaging-sms-whatsapp.md`. Note: text-only converts less than call+text — use it when calls genuinely won't happen (you or staff won't dial), or when converting <15% of leads with the current approach. *(gym-launch/09)*

## Working the pipeline like the best 1%

- **BAMFAM: Book A Meeting From A Meeting.** Never end any contact without the next contact scheduled. A prospect should never be in no-man's-land. "Let's circle back offline" = resolve the time obstacle *now*, calendars open. *(yt/03)*
- **On-call SOP and off-call SOP.** Most teams only script the conversation. The best also have a checklist for the gaps: follow-ups, pulling appointments forward, canceling dead slots, working the kill list. The off-call work is what fills the calendar that the on-call skill converts. *(yt/03)*
- **Keep a kill list.** Your highest-value prospects, visible somewhere you look every day — outside the CRM — so they get deliberate daily attention. *(yt/03)*
- **Don't take rejection personally; work leads harder.** Outreach attempts per lead correlate directly with calls taken. People who can't handle a no-show or a nasty reply stop working the list and lose silently. *(yt/03)*
- **Be a trash man — yellows are gold.** If you're new or under-booked, take the leads nobody wants. Less-qualified leads are paid-for practice with real stakes: "if I can close someone with no money and no job, I've gotten really good at this skill." Treat warm-up reps like heavy reps or you're practicing being bad. And people who "look poor" often just dress different — more times than can be counted. *(yt/03, yt/01)*
- **Never blame circumstances.** "The leads are bad" is a dog's excuse. Take 100% control of everything controllable; if lead quality data genuinely shifted, report it once, privately, neutrally, while still working them. *(yt/03)*
- **Ask for referrals at the moment of yes.** Not "do you know anyone?" (yes/no) but **"Who do you know** — who's as awesome as you — who'd also benefit from this?" (forces a name, wrapped in a compliment). With rapport, the playful escalation: *"Why do you hate your friends? …I can only assume you hate them because you don't want them to know about this amazing thing. And if it's because you don't believe me — let's talk about that."* One new rep outperformed a whole team ~50% on identical scripts purely from end-of-call referral asks. *(yt/03)*

## Prep: how to out-research the competition

- **Rule of thumb: prep ≈ 10% of meeting length** for routine calls (60-min call → 6 minutes of prep). For rare/high-stakes presentations it inverts (1 hour → ~6 hours). *(yt/02)*
- **You win through preparation when you don't yet have a reputation.** When cold-outreaching strangers: assemble a mini-profile (their socials, site, visible problems), open with a *specific observed problem and your proposed solution* — "I saw your square-footage layout; if you moved X you could double usable space — I don't know if you're open to that, but I thought about it before we got on the phone because I don't want to waste your time." Knowing about them ≈ being known by them: it approximates friendship, and people like and trust people who know more about them. *(yt/03)*
- **Take notes; open follow-ups with them.** "So — daughters Sarah and Jessica, finishing at Colorado, X and Y happening in the business — did I get that right? Just reviewed my notes before the call." Now every recommendation you make is assumed to be contextual — which converts you from salesperson to consultant. Pass the notes to whoever serves the customer next. *(yt/03)*
- The five-second version works anywhere: meeting a girlfriend's father, look up his company in the car and ask about the press release. Respect through demonstrated homework. *(yt/03)*
