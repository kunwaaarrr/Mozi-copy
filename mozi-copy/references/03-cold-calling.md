# 03 — Cold Calling & Phone Scripts

The exact phone playbook: the opener, the goal (book them same-day/next-day), and the overcomes for everything people say to get off the phone. Cadence/timing rules are in `02-outreach-and-nurture.md`; tonality and delivery in `08-delivery-voice-mindset.md`.

> Phone calls here are *booking* calls — get a time, get them excited, get off in under 3 minutes. The full sales conversation is `05-sales-conversation.md`.

## The opener (The Script)

**Verbatim (fitness original):**
> "Sarah?" *(just the name — excited, like you know her)*
> "…yeah, who's this?"
> "This is Alex." *(pause — act like she should know you)*
> "This is Alex… from 123 Fitness, in Orange County. I'm just calling because you signed up for our free six-week challenge on Facebook."
> "Oh, yeah, yeah—"
> "We had two spots open up, so I'm calling off the sold-out list to see what time you can come in **tonight**. I'm here till 8."
*(gym-launch/06)*

**Why each beat works:**
- **Name only — never "Is this Sarah? I'm looking for…"** That instantly flags telemarketer (nobody else answers your cell). Saying just their name with energy makes them rack their brain like they should know you, which buys the next 15 seconds.
- **Local + familiar + source**: business name, area, and *where they signed up* ("on Facebook") — three recognition anchors that answer "is this legit?" before it's asked.
- **Scarcity** ("two spots opened up, sold-out list") **+ urgency** (tonight) **+ assume the sale** ("what time can you come in" — never "would you like to").
- **"I'm here till 8."** People assume businesses close at 5. Giving the data flips "I can't make it today" into "oh, you're open till 8? 6:30 works great." Tag it onto every ask.

**Generalized template:**
> "[Name]?" → "This is [you], from [business] in [their area]. I'm calling because you [exact thing they did — downloaded, signed up, requested] on [platform]. A couple of spots just opened up [for X], so I'm seeing what time works for you **today** — I'm available till [time]."

**The goal is always same-day/next-day.** Show rates fall off a cliff beyond that. If they push out: *"When were you planning on [doing the thing — working out / reviewing this]?"* → "after work" → *"Cool — let's just do that time tomorrow."* It works far more often than it should. *(gym-launch/08)*

## Scarcity that's real: the Chris & Robin story

A couple ran sales for a month-long launch (170+ sales in 24 days), hitting the scarcity lines the whole time — show rate stuck around 50%. The last three days, when the deadline was *actually* real, they said it like they meant it: *"You have to make it in by Friday, or you can't get a spot. If you miss your appointment tonight, you won't be able to do the program — it's closed."* Show rate: **70%+**. Identical words; the difference was conviction. The lesson for any copy: scarcity moves people exactly to the degree the writer believes it. Make deadlines real, then state them flatly. And mathematically, fighting for the *first* appointment beats allowing reschedules: a reschedule means more work *and* a lower show probability than the original booking. *(gym-launch/06)*

## Call overcomes (the "give me 30 seconds" library)

Head game first: **if they had time to pick up and tell you they're busy, they have time to tell you what time they can come in.** Genuinely busy people silence unknown numbers. "I'm busy" is a complaint, not an objection — acknowledge it and steamroll cheerfully. *(gym-launch/07)*

| They say | You say (verbatim core) | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm busy / at work / call me later" | "No problem, totally understand — just want to know what time you want to come in tonight. I'm here till 8. Can you make 6:30?" | The question "when should I call back?" and "when can you come in?" require the same mental act — generating a free time — so skip to the one you want. Name a specific time (if they're at work, they're off by 5–6 → suggest 6:30/7). |
| "I'm with my kids" | "No problem — we're kid friendly! What time do you want to come in tonight? I'm here till 8." | Removes the hidden logistics objection (arranging childcare). And a kid present at the sale is rapport material, not a problem. |
| "I'm too old / out of shape" (vibe) | "Hey, we're beginner friendly. So what time tonight? I'm here till 8." | Same pattern: name the fear, kill it in four words, re-ask. |
| "Just tell me the price" (with rapport) | "Slow down partner — we just met, we're still on appetizers and you're already trying to take me home! We'll get there." | Humor resets the frame; laughter buys time. |
| "Just tell me the price" (insistent) | "Honestly — I **can't** tell you the price over the phone. What I **can** tell you is we had 14 people sign up yesterday, four already today, and we close tonight. What time can you come in? I'm here till 8." | The "**I can't, but I can**" close: honest about the catch, substitutes social proof + scarcity for the withheld info. |
| The talker (won't get off the phone) | "Hey [name] — is this your cell?" → "Yes" → "Awesome — mind if I just shoot you a text with all the details, address, all that good stuff?" → "Sure" → "Doing it right now. Can't wait to see you tomorrow!" *boop* | Works at any point in their monologue; they say yes twice and the call is over. **No booking call should pass 3 minutes.** |
| "Tell me more about the program" (detail-seeker) | Thousand-foot overview, fast, no gaps for questions: "You'll be lifting weights, doing cardio, we'll build your meal plan, grocery list, food prep — it's awesome, everyone loves it. What time can you come in?" | Details will not make them show up; curiosity will. If they're really curious, they can come to the appointment. (See Law 11 — details are where you die.) |

*(gym-launch/07)*

## Booking-call edification (when you book for someone else)

When the person who calls isn't the person who'll sell, **edify the closer**: *"I'm going to put you on with Sean — Sean's unbelievable. He's helped 400 people just like you get [the thing you just said you wanted] — recently he helped someone in your exact situation do [X]. You're going to love talking to him. …Ooh, you know what, I think he has an opening this afternoon — let me see if I can get you in, hold on two seconds. [pause] Yep, he confirmed."* Borrowed authority + live scarcity + same-day pull-up in one move. *(yt/01)*

Also seed reputation before it's earned: *"Hey, before we get going — did you hear about us from the [city's best X] list? …No? Oh, from the Facebook ad — got it."* They now know about the list either way. The list never brings business; *telling people about the list* brings business. *(yt/01)*

And disarm with humor: *"I just wanted to make sure that, one, you weren't a crazy person on the internet — ha — and to find a time that works for you to come in. And obviously to show you that we're not crazy people either."* *(yt/01)*

## True cold outreach (they've never heard of you)

When there's no opt-in: build a mini-profile first (their website, socials, anything public), then open with a **specific observed problem plus your thought-through solution**, e.g.: *"I looked at your [site/space/profile] — I think if you moved [X] you could [specific gain]. I don't know if you're open to something like that, but I worked it out before reaching out because I don't want to waste your time figuring things out live."* You win through preparation when you don't yet have reputation. Full prep system in `02-outreach-and-nurture.md`. *(yt/03)*
